THE SCIENTIFIC RESULT · H58 · 2026-10-10

Damage-zone width grows with mapped length, but very sub-linearly — and the handed Riedel term is not supported.

Fitted width law. On a fresh buffered whole-component draw (seed 2026, 11,360 withheld positives), the 90th-percentile distance of withheld strands to their nearest visible trace was regressed against mapped-component length across 6 bins: gamma = 0.154, 95% CI [0.0732, 0.2882]. The interval excludes 1, so widening is strongly sub-linear in this proxy — consistent with damage-zone growth that decelerates with displacement (Savage & Brodsky 2011) and not with a constant-ratio halo. Absolute half-widths are 20.2 px at L0 = 40 px, i.e. kilometres, not tens of metres.

Structure the data does not show. Signed radial obliquity of withheld pixels around their host, and its interaction with the recorded slip sense, produced no enriched bin (all enrichment ≤ 0.007 against a base rate of 0.0023), and the left/right ratio by host sense is +0.096 / -0.153 nats — noise. The candidate arm that adds those columns therefore beats the reference by 0.0017 with 95% CI [-0.0037, 0.0078]; the arm ships only if that interval clears zero (retained: False). Axial relative strike, by contrast, is real: withheld medians 13.6° versus a 6.4° visible-null.

Allocation. The dot budget is the lane rule, not a tuned number: 21,748 ceiling = 4 × 10,000 × the fitted fraction of withheld positives inside the kept (distance × strike) zone, 0.5437. Dots are kept > 2 px off the mapped catalogue because organiser thread 11516 fully penalises that flank; on this instrument that policy is untestable (the 3 px context collar already emptied it, so the flank effect measures 0.0000). The budget shape is inherited from a re-measured registry fact, not taste: across 15 owner-scored rasters in this repository, dot count correlates with the reported score at Spearman -0.810 (p = 0.00025) and median distance-to-catalogue at 0.766 — confounded by construction, since method and budget change together, so it sets a prior about density, not a target.
