Why 0.2778 won, and whether higher is achievable

Substituting the binary algebra, DTI = T / (0.2(T + n - M) + 0.8K), where T is covered truth credit, n the dot count, M the dots' total self-credit and K the number of true new-fault pixels. Two things follow.

1. The ceiling is 1.0, not 0.5556. A perfect prediction has n = M = T = K, so D = 0.2K + 0.8K = K and DTI = 1. Verified against the brute-force implementation. (0.05556 is a different number: it is the marginal acceptance bar alpha * DTI at DTI = 0.2778.)

2. At the real base rate, DTI is close to the covered fraction of the truth — but only while the dot budget stays comparable to K. The grid is sparse: with ~5,660 true pixels in ~2.56 M cells the base rate is 0.00221, and a random pixel lands within 3 px of a true one only 0.0207 of the time. Read the row at coverage 0.2778: the same coverage is worth 0.3162 at half the truth count in dots and only 0.1809 at six times it. That is the whole story of 0.2778. It is roughly 28% coverage of the live truth set achieved at a near-matched dot budget — and the reason nothing sprayed more dots beat it is that beyond n ≈ 2K every extra dot costs 0.2 in the denominator while returning far less than 0.2 in credit.

Is higher achievable? Yes, and the route is arithmetic rather than clever: DTI tracks coverage roughly one-for-one while n ≲ 2K, so the 0.3774 high-water mark implies about 40% coverage at a matched budget. Beating 0.2778 therefore needs roughly ten points more coverage at the same budget. This lane measured a real coverage gain over distance-only (0.1425 DTI, mode all; PENDING mode detached, with disjoint confidence intervals), which is the right direction — but on withheld catalogue pixels, which cling to visible traces. Whether that transfers to genuinely uncatalogued faults is not knowable from here, and the +0.14 holdout-to-live rank correlation says do not assume it does. No projected live score is claimed anywhere on this site.
