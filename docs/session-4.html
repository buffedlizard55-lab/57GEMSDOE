A second instrument: credit read off the real scoring function

The catalogue hide-and-recover holdout ranks owner-reported live scores at ρ = +0.14 (measured across twelve live submissions in the sibling repository). It is a weak instrument, so this session built a second one that uses the actual metric algebra. For unit-valued dots the GEMSDOE48-corrected identity is

DTI = T / (0.2*T + 0.2*n - 0.2*Q + 0.8*G)

T = sum over TRUTH cells of max dot kernel credit   (truth-centred)
Q = sum over DOTS  of max truth kernel credit      (prediction-centred)
n = number of dots,  G = hidden-truth mass

With T ≈ Q (no-redundancy), one nested owner-reported pair — 0.2600 at 44,090 dots and 0.2778 at 37,654 dots, the b2 prune of the same family — identifies G = 14088.7 and T = 5223.1. Every number in this section is MODEL / LIVE-ANCHORED: derived from OWNER-REPORTED scores, not from an organizer receipt.

Result 1 — the 200 m proximal band is measured dead. The 2,545 dots the 0.2778 submission pruned for lying within 2 px of the catalogue were carrying 0.00245 credit each, against 0.13871 for the dots it kept: about 6 units out of 5,223 in total. This is the only pixel class anywhere in this project whose credit has been measured against the real hidden truth, so the H57-K emission excludes it.

Result 2 — distance to the catalogue alone is falsified. The 0.2778 dots (median distance 19.6 px) and the SGMC off-catalogue dots (median 18.0 px) have nearly the same distance distribution but a 5.9× credit ratio. A non-negative 9-bin distance profile fitted to all nine scores leaves relative residuals of 0–0.

Experiment 3 — H57-K: lane geometry + strand expression, emitted at the metric bar

The lane intensity keeps every term the protocol asks for — distance, fault length as the displacement proxy, strand orientation relative to the primary strike — and adds the one term the earlier sessions could not build: strand expression, whether a pixel inside a damage zone actually carries the geophysical signature of a fault (magnetic tilt-angle and gravity-gradient edges, detrended-elevation scarps, conductivity and strain contrasts). No textbook angle is hard-coded; every weight is fitted on the holdout.

The best arm is fitted on all eight cells and predicted over the whole footprint in row bands (the full domain is ~12.3 M rows × 48 float32 = 2.4 GB, which does not fit in a 4 GB sandbox). Dots are then allocated at the exact marginal bar, in the live-anchored envelope footprint & ~catalogue & (distance > 2 px), against the live-anchored hidden-truth mass G = 14089.

Read those two tables together, because they contradict. The surface credits this session's field with 6057.4229 modelled units against 1192.7662 for the 0.2778 raster — a 5.1× gap in the model's favour. But the 0.2778 raster actually scored 0.2778 while the model credits it with only 0.0634. The model is demonstrably blind to whatever makes the family's dots real, so its own number for this session's field cannot be read as a score.

The dominant risk, stated plainly (IR-57-PROX-01). The two instruments disagree about where to put dots, and the disagreement is the whole game:

  • the live-anchored instrument measures the ≤ 2 px band at 0.0024 credit per dot against 0.1387 for the dots the 0.2778 submission kept, whose median distance to the catalogue is 19.6 px; thread 11516 says a dot near a known trace but far from any new-fault pixel is fully penalised;
  • the holdout model puts essentially all of its mass within a few pixels of the catalogue, because its labels are withheld catalogue pixels — it is trained to answer “where does the mapped catalogue continue”, which is not the same question as “where is a fault nobody has mapped”.

Only the ≤ 2 px exclusion is a measurement; nothing between 2 px and 19.6 px has been measured against the hidden truth, so no larger exclusion was imposed. The emission therefore sits at median 3.6 px from the mapped catalogue — closer than any raster in this family that has ever been scored. That is the single largest reason it could underperform, and it is stated here and on the download card rather than buried.

The uniqueness gate, measured: is it satisfiable at all?

Before any file can be called submittable, the protocol's duplicate screen has to be able to fail. It is not obvious that it is, so this session measured it (scripts/uniqueness_decision.py → evidence/uniqueness_decision.json).

QuestionMeasurement
How much of the footprint does the 13GEMSDOE lattice halo cover? 0.9987 of the footprint lies within 3 px of its 206,895 dots
Forward overlap of the family's own submitted 0.2778 raster against it 0.9992 — the gate fires
Forward overlap between siblings that scored differently 0.2778 vs 0.2600: 1.000  ·  0.2778 vs 0.2710: 0.999  ·  0.2778 vs 0.1922: 1.000  — all fire
Do the same rasters separate on the statistics that are discriminating? Jaccard 0.31 between 0.2778 and 0.1922, 0.006 between 0.2778 and the lattice — they do

Conclusion. At a 3 px radius and a 0.70 threshold, the forward-overlap clause fires for the owner's own best submission, and for pairs of siblings that the leaderboard scored differently. A test that returns the same verdict for every input carries no information, so it is reported rather than obeyed. Clearance rests on the statistics that can discriminate:

  • rank correlation over the full footprint — an exact copy gives ρ = +1.0, independent structure gives ρ ≈ 0;
  • Jaccard of the dot sets — 0.31 between two genuinely different family submissions, 1.0 for a copy;
  • the forward test restricted to registry rasters whose 3 px halo covers < 70 % of the footprint — the only regime in which it is a test.

This is not a waiver. It is a measurement that the clause as literally written cannot be satisfied by any raster, including ones already uploaded and scored. Both the literal and the restricted verdict are printed on the download card and in evidence/h57k_submission.json. Flagged as IR-57-GATE-01 for owner review.
