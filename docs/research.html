Research — hypothesis, evidence, run card

Hypothesis (this lane)

Expert-mapped new faults (the hidden truth) concentrate as secondary strands in the damage zones of known USGS/INGENIOUS faults: subparallel splays and parallel strands whose distance from the primary trace, azimuth relative to its strike, and along-strike position (beyond-tip extensions) follow shear-zone mechanics, with zone width growing with fault length (displacement proxy). The organizers confirmed a new fault is any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS, including newly mapped geometry of an existing system, so splays and parallel strands count (thread 11536).

Mechanism

Per-fault intensity I(p) = f(d/s(L)) * g(phi), where f is the empirical radial density, g the empirical offset-azimuth density, and s(L) the empirical length scaling (displacement proxy), ALL fitted on the hide-and-recover holdout (random whole-segment folds, 300 m truth buffer, features rebuilt from visible faults only, visible+INGENIOUS faults masked pixel-exactly, pooled DTI alpha=0.2 beta=0.8 R=300 m). No textbook Riedel/Tchalenko angle is hard-coded. Sense of slip is conditioned where the INGENIOUS database records it (RL/LL/N) and kept marginal where it does not (the holdout cannot resolve it: nearest-visible sense unk=38063, N=214, RL=53, LL=9). Emission: binary top-k dots off known faults, k swept on the holdout. The d<3 px band (removed by the holdout's 300 m truth buffer) is extended from the SGMC off-catalogue population and flagged as such.

Named non-fault process that could mimic it

Anthropogenic and geomorphic lineaments that parallel mapped faults in the alluvial basins of the northwestern Great Basin: roads, fence lines and powerline alignments, and pluvial-lake shorelines / alluvial-fan risers that follow basin structure subparallel to fault strikes. These cluster near and parallel to mapped fault scarps and would be predicted by the same halo geometry without being faults.
