What the published scores actually tell us about this metric

We measured the registry rasters instead of trusting prose. The owner-reported best file in this family (37,654 dots) is a strict subset of its 40,199-dot base (True): it adds nothing and deletes 2,545 dots, all of them within 2.00 px of the mapped catalogue, while every kept dot sits at least 2.24 px away — and the deletion is reproduced exactly by a 2 px rule (True). Under the official metric a catalogue-adjacent dot with no new-fault pixel within 3 px buys zero coverage and pays 0.2 × p in false-positive cost, so that prune is arithmetic, not luck.

Across the scored registry the same direction repeats (rank-correlation table on the research page), but method and dot budget change together there, so it is description with n = 15 and no causal control. It is why this release keeps dots off the catalogue flank and caps its budget. It is not a claim about our file's future score.
